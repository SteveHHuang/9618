In 1973, Karl von Frisch won the Nobel Prize for decoding 
the 'waggle dance' of honeybees. This intricate movement
pattern, performed by scout bees inside the hive,
communicates the location of food sources to other
workers. The dance's angle correlates with the sun's
position, while its duration indicates distance.
Recent studies at Sussex University revealed that
bees adjust their dance based on wind conditions—a nuance
 previously undocumented.
